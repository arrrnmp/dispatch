EU's AI Act Enforcement Begins With First Compliance Audits

The European Commission has initiated formal compliance reviews at three major foundation model providers, reportedly including a US-headquartered lab and two European startups. The audits mark the first enforcement action under the AI Act, which entered its operational phase in March 2026.

Commissioners emphasized that the reviews are standard procedure and not punitive, but industry watchers note the symbolism: Brussels is signaling it will move faster than many expected. The three providers have 30 days to submit documentation covering training data provenance, bias testing, and risk mitigation protocols.

The move has already drawn mixed reactions. European AI startups largely welcomed the clarity; US trade groups called it regulatory overreach that threatens transatlantic cooperation.

Reuters Politico EU FT
Dispatch Note

The EU has a pattern of writing ambitious laws and then slowly figuring out enforcement. What's different here is the speed: these audits came just six weeks after the compliance deadline. Either Brussels is serious this time, or someone wants to make an early example.

Apple Reveals On-Device LLM Strategy at WWDC

Apple unveiled a 3-billion parameter language model running entirely on the new A20 chip, with no cloud calls, no API keys, no data leaving the device. The model powers a new Apple Intelligence layer across iOS, macOS, and visionOS, handling summarization, drafting, and contextual suggestions in real time.

Benchmarks shared on-stage show the model matching GPT-4-mini on several reasoning benchmarks while running at 28 tokens per second on-device. The pitch is straightforward: privacy is the product, and speed is the proof. Developers can access the model through a new local inference API with no usage fees.

The move puts Apple in direct competition with Google's Gemini Nano and Samsung's Gauss, but Apple's advantage is ecosystem control. Every iPhone 18 ships with the same silicon, meaning no fragmentation.

The Verge Bloomberg Ars Technica 9to5Mac
Dispatch Note

3B parameters on-device is impressive, but the real story is the API. Apple is telling developers: build on our model, run on our silicon, pay us nothing for inference. It is a land grab disguised as a feature release. If they can make the model good enough for 80% of use cases, the cloud-based AI vendors lose their easiest revenue stream.
